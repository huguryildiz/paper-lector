# Position

Turn what was actually read into a related-work section that defends a specific result. Positioning comes after the result, because synthesizing literature first is how coverage-table discovery starts again.

## Precondition

Ask for:

1. a stated claim with its result, of any kind: quantitative, theoretical or negative;
2. the evidence behind it;
3. reading notes or PDFs of the works to position against, including the closest papers from the novelty check.

Without a claim and result, say so and produce at most a one-page reading map, labelled as such.

## Procedure

1. **Write the contribution in one sentence**, with its condition: "Under [conditions], we establish [result] relative to [prior result or comparator], supported by [evidence]." Everything below serves that sentence.
2. **Build a positioning table with assumption or result rows, not feature columns** ([template](../templates/comparison-table.md)). Rows are the assumptions the contribution keeps or breaks and the results it changes: bound, guarantee, counterexample, evidence, validity range. A new result under the same assumptions differs on a result row. Columns are the compared works plus this work. Cells state the work's assumption or result, with DOI and page or equation where seen, tagged full text, abstract only or unverified. Never fill a cell from a word's absence; unknown stays unknown.
3. **Group works by the assumption or result row where they differ from this work**, not by venue, year or method label. Each group becomes one paragraph.
4. **Write the prose.** Per paragraph: what the group does, one sentence per work with its specific assumption or result, then the sentence stating what this work does differently and why it matters for the decision. Match each verb to the strength of the source's claim.
5. **Give method-side neighbours their own paragraph**: the literature where the same structure appears under another name. Reviewers from the author's own community look for it.
6. **Cite the author's own prior work** as lineage in one or two sentences, not as a paragraph.
7. **Audit every citation**: the DOI resolves, authors, year and venue match, the page or equation was seen. List abstract-only and unverified items at the end, and which two or three works should still be read in full.

## Rules

- Compare formulations, not vocabulary. A shared word in a title is not an overlap.
- Scope every gap statement to the corpus: "among the papers we read, as of this date, none gives X under Y". Avoid "first", "novel" and "comprehensive" without a basis, and "to the best of our knowledge, no work has" unless the novelty check supports that exact statement.
- No scores, originality rankings or novelty certificates.
- Do not expand the corpus. If a missing comparator appears, name it and stop; reading it is the author's decision.
- A gap statement supports "novel" at most. "Original" needs the conditions in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).
